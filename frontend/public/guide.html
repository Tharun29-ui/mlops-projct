WORKSPACE GUIDE

From bills to a useful business plan.

Start with the source documents. Review once, then let saved transactions update stock and feed the models.

1. Add purchase bills

In Invoices, choose Purchase when uploading. Open each original beside the extracted details. Check its date, invoice number, HSN, quantities and net amounts. Mark delivery, labour and licence charges appropriately so they do not become physical stock.

2. Confirm product matches and approve

Match the exact brand, model, capacity and unit. Shared HSN codes do not mean two products are the same. Approving a purchase adds stock automatically. Approving a goods sale reduces it. Duplicates and historical stock shortages need correction first.

3. Record a sale

Use Sales to select products, quantities and rates, or upload an existing sales invoice. The app records the sale, reduces stock and provides a sales receipt. Stock adjustments are for confirmed corrections or opening counts, not routine sales.

4. Explore the 20 reviewed real bills

Open Forecasts → Real bills (10 + 10). The real purchase images and sales PDFs have been checked against their originals. Expand a bill for its corrected table. Choose Train & evaluate these 20 bills to rerun the sample analysis; download the model and results.

This selected sample is incomplete. Its model estimates the value of a recorded invoice, not daily demand. It does not invent missing sales, opening stock or historical profit. Check the test error before interpreting any estimate.

5. Forecast daily product sales

In Sales forecast, use complete approved real sales history or a daily CSV. Include every calendar day; only confirm zero sales where records establish there were none. Choose the period and generate a forecast.

6. Compare costs and pricing

In Costs & pricing, choose reviewed real bills, approved business records, or the larger unverified dataset. Search a product. The app compares comparable purchase and sale prices. Sparse history uses a reference price. Scenario profit is selling rate minus cost times your chosen quantity, before other expenses; it is not a separately learned profit forecast.

7. Read the test results

Lower WAPE and MAE mean less error. Methods are selected on earlier validation data and checked on later test data. A few test invoices cannot prove business accuracy. Selected-invoice analysis and complete daily history are evaluated separately; a failed baseline check means the experimental model is unsuitable for business decisions.
